When food manufacturers start looking for technology solutions, someone inevitably says "we need an ERP." It makes sense on the surface — one system for everything. But here's the problem: ERP systems are built for accounting and financial management, not for running a food manufacturing plant.

## What ERP Systems Do Well

ERPs are excellent at financial reporting, accounts payable and receivable, general ledger management, and purchase order processing. They give your CFO exactly what they need.

## What ERP Systems Don't Do Well

They don't capture real-time data from a scale on your receiving dock. They don't integrate with the PLCs controlling your process lines. They don't track lot-level traceability from ingredient to finished good with the granularity that SQF and FSMA require. They don't generate customer-specific shipping labels or transmit ASNs.

Most critically, ERP systems don't understand the *flow* of a food manufacturing operation — the way raw materials become ingredients become batches become products become pallets become shipments. They see transactions. Your plant floor needs something that sees *process*.

## The Operations Platform Approach

An operations platform is built from the plant floor up, not from the general ledger down. It starts with the physical reality of your operation:

- **Receiving**: What showed up? What condition is it in? Does it meet spec?
- **Production**: What are we making? What went into it? What happened during the process?
- **Quality**: Does it meet standards? Can we prove it? Can we trace it?
- **Warehouse**: Where is it? How old is it? What's its status?
- **Shipping**: What's going out? To whom? With what documentation?

Each of these areas connects to the others, creating a continuous chain of data from dock to dock. The ERP still handles finance — but the operations platform handles everything that happens on the plant floor.

## Why This Matters

When an auditor asks for a trace, they don't want a financial transaction history. They want to know which supplier lot went into which batch, what the quality results were, where the finished product is stored, and which customers received it.

That's not an ERP question. That's an operations question. And it requires an operations platform to answer.

## The Practical Reality

You probably already have an ERP. Keep it. It's good at what it does. But stop trying to make it do what it was never designed for. Instead, build a connected operations layer that handles the plant floor — and connect it to your ERP for the financial pieces.
