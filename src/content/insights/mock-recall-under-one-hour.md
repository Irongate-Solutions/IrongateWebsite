Mock recalls are the single best indicator of whether your plant's traceability systems actually work. Not on paper. Not in theory. In practice, under pressure, with real data.

## The Standard You Should Be Aiming For

A mock recall should take less than an hour. Not three days. Not a weekend. Under an hour — from the moment someone says "trace this lot" to the moment a complete, audit-ready report is on the table.

That means knowing, within minutes:
- Every raw material lot that went into a finished product
- Every finished product lot that contained a specific raw material
- Where those products are now — in storage, in transit, or at a customer

## Why Most Plants Can't Do This

The honest answer: because data lives in too many places. Receiving records are in one system. Lab results are in another. Production batch records might be on paper. Warehouse locations are tracked in a spreadsheet — if they're tracked at all.

When someone says "trace ingredient X from supplier Y," the team has to manually stitch together information from four or five different sources. That takes time. And under audit pressure, it creates errors.

## What "Good" Looks Like

In a well-connected operation, a mock recall looks like this:

1. Enter the lot number or ingredient identifier
2. The system traces forward (what finished products used this ingredient?) and backward (what ingredients went into this product?)
3. A complete report generates automatically — lot numbers, dates, quantities, current locations, customer shipments
4. Total elapsed time: 15–30 minutes

No running between departments. No pulling binders. No emergency meetings.
